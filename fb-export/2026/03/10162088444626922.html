Hashem Al-Ghaili:

European colonization killed so many Native Americans, it triggered a mini ice age .

In the wake of European arrival in the Americas, a catastrophic event known as the "Great Dying" decimated nearly 90 percent of the Indigenous population. Using census data, archaeological records, and ice core samples, researchers from University College London estimated that 56 million people perished within a century, primarily due to introduced pathogens. This massive loss of life led to the immediate abandonment of approximately 138 million acres of managed farmland, leaving vast territories to be reclaimed by fast-growing forests and savannas.

This rapid reforestation acted as a massive carbon sink, pulling enough carbon dioxide from the atmosphere to lower global temperatures. Scientists found that carbon levels dropped by 7-10 parts per million, a shift large enough to trigger the coldest phase of the "Little Ice Age." While natural processes played a role, researchers argue that this human-driven ecological shift was essential to the period's extreme cooling. The study underscores how historical genocide left a measurable, chilling mark on the Earth’s climate system centuries before the modern industrial era.

source: Koch, A., Brierley, C., Maslin, M. M., & Lewis, S. L. Earth system impacts of the European arrival and Great Dying in the Americas after 1492. Quaternary Science Reviews.
