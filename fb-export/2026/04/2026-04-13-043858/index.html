State Level Referendum: Proposed Procedure for Making Gun Keeping Mandatory for Decent Citizens
(State Wide Referendum: Right to bear Gun to Law Abide Citizens)
Law’s Summary: The Chief Minister will implement this law only when in the referendum at least 55% of the total voters of the state give clear consent to implement it. After passing in the referendum, this law will give every citizen of the state the right to keep a gun. All cases related to this law will be disposed of by citizens’ jury and not by judge, and the jury will be able to impose a ban or penalty on any citizen for possessing a gun. Along with this, for citizens keeping property worth more than 10 lakh it will be mandatory to keep at least 1 gun and 100 cartridges. The Prime Minister can implement this law in any state or any district with the consent of 55% voters of that state. The Chief Minister can also implement this law in one or more districts of his state. #StateGunLawRrp #RRP16
Section 1: Instructions for Citizens and Officers
01 This law will come in the gazette and then a referendum will be held in the state. If at least 55% voters of the total voters registered in the state’s voter list register “Yes” to implement this law, only then this law will be implemented, otherwise not.
1. In the referendum the voter will have only the option to register ‘Yes’ or ‘No’, and before the referendum is completed the Chief Minister will not make any change in any section of this law. After this law comes into force, if any voter of the state wants any partial or full change in any section of this law, then he can submit an affidavit for such change in the District Collector office.
2. The Collector will accept the voter’s affidavit by charging a fee at the rate of 20 Rs per page, and will scan it and put it on the Chief Minister’s website so that other voters can register their consent on that affidavit.
3. If 55% citizens registered in the state’s voter list register Yes on that affidavit then the Chief Minister can issue orders to implement the suggestions given in the affidavit.
4. The Chief Minister or Prime Minister can suspend this law for 4 years in any district with the approval of 51% voters of the state, and the Prime Minister can suspend this law in Gujarat with the approval of 51% voters of India.

02 In this law, adult citizen means – a citizen whose age is more than 22 years. This law does not give permission to citizens below 22 years of age to possess a gun. Citizens below 22 years of age will remain outside the scope of this law in every respect.
03 After this law comes into force, any adult citizen of the state can keep any registered (Registered) gun of small, medium or large size with him.
1. For keeping a gun, the citizen will have to get his gun registered with the District Gun Officer.
2. Any citizen can keep 2 guns of any category with him.
3. For keeping more than two guns, the citizen will have to take a license from the District Police Chief.
04 If any adult citizen is the owner of property worth more than 10 lakh then it will be mandatory for him to keep the following number of guns and cartridges in his house:
1. Property worth more than 10 lakh – one small gun and 100 cartridges
2. Property worth more than 20 lakh – one medium sized gun and 100 cartridges
3. Property worth more than 30 lakh – one large sized gun and 100 cartridges
4. In the property, 1 house of value up to 1 crore and furniture up to 10 lakh will not be included.
05 All disputes related to this law will be heard by citizens’ jury. If your name comes out in the lottery then you can be called for jury duty. In the jury, you will have to listen to the debate by seeing the evidence presented by the accused, victim, witnesses and lawyers of both sides and give a decision of punishment/fine or release.
Section II: Instructions for Officers
06 The Chief Minister will appoint one District Gun Officer (DGO = District Gun Officer) in every district. The District Gun Officer and his staff will be within the scope of vote recall and jury panel. See Section 14 for vote recall.
07 The District Gun Officer will publish the list of guns in the following categories for classifying pistols, revolvers, rifles, machine guns, carbines etc. guns:
1. Types of small guns.
2. Types of medium sized guns.
3. Types of large guns.
4. Cartridges, bullets and their types.
08 The District Gun Officer will determine the rules for mandatory training and optional training for all citizens for operating guns and their maintenance.
1. Upon declaration of the mandatory training programme, it will be mandatory for all citizens aged 22 to 50 years living in the district to take training within 2 years.
2. DGO can obtain the necessary funds for running training camps from the Finance Minister etc., or can accept grants, donations etc. Training fee will be payable by the trainee, and the rates of training fee will be decided by the District Gun Officer.
3. DGO will select 0.01% adult citizens from the voter list by lottery every week in the presence of the Grand Jury panel. DGO or the employee appointed by him will go to the houses of the citizens selected by lottery and ensure that those citizens are keeping guns and cartridges according to the prescribed rules or not.

09 The Government of India will make public the designs of INSAS rifle, 303, 202, .22 revolver, all the guns used by Indian police which are of ‘INSAS or lower’ level. Any citizen can set up a factory in the state to manufacture guns, gun parts, cartridges, bullet-proof jackets according to this design without any license, only by getting registration.
10 If any person in the state wants to set up a manufacturing unit, factory etc. for making guns then he can start the factory by getting his registration done with the District Gun Officer. No license or permission from any department will be required to start the factory.
• After this law comes into force, foreign investment in gun manufacturing will not be allowed in the state, and only units with 100% Indian citizen ownership will be able to establish gun manufacturing factories.
11 Grand Jury; Appointment of Jury Administrator and Formation of Grand Jury Panel
(11.1) The District Gun Officer will appoint one District Jury Administrator in every district. If citizens are not satisfied with the work of the Jury Administrator then they can give consent to change the Jury Administrator by using the vote recall process.
(11.2) Formation of the first Grand Jury Panel: The District Jury Administrator will select 50 voters aged 25 to 50 years from the district’s voter list by lottery in a public meeting. After taking interviews of these members the Jury Administrator can remove any 20 members. In this way 30 Grand Jury members will remain.
(11.3) Subsequent Grand Jury Panels: From the first Grand Jury Panel the District Jury Administrator will retire the first 10 Grand Jury members every 10 days. After the first month every Grand Jury member’s term will be of 3 months, therefore 10 Grand Jury members will retire every month and 10 new ones will be selected. For selecting the new 10 members the Jury Administrator will select 20 members by lottery from the district voter list and will remove any 10 of them through interview.
1. Grand Jury members will hold meetings every Saturday and Sunday. If a meeting is held then it should start at 11 am and end by 4 pm. Jury members will get 600 Rs per attendance and travel expenses.
2. If a private sector employee is called for jury duty then the employer will give him unpaid leave for the required days. The employer can deduct the salary for the leave days from the employee’s salary.
3. All categories of government employees will remain completely outside the scope of jury duty.
4. Citizens who have done jury duty will not be called for jury duty for the next 10 years.
12 Territorial Jurisdiction of the Jury Panel
(12.1) The jury can prohibit any unsound person from keeping a gun for a specified period. After the said period expires another jury panel will decide whether the accused should be given permission to keep a gun or not.
(12.2) DGO or any citizen can give the Grand Jury Panel a list of people who should be prohibited from keeping a gun. If the Grand Jury Panel approves it, then a new jury panel will be formed. If 67% members of the jury panel give consent then that person will be prohibited from keeping a gun.
(12.3) If any sound citizen is not keeping a gun or cartridges in his house as per the prescribed rules, then the Grand Jury Panel members will decide whether the case should be heard or not.
(12.4) The final authority to decide whether a person is sound or unsound will rest with the jury. The jury will decide on the basis of the person’s criminal record, conduct, allegations, conviction etc. using its own discretion.
(12.5) The District Jury Administrator will also get all complaints against the District Gun Officer heard by the jury.

13 Hearing by the Jury Panel
(13.1) If there is any dispute related to the cases given in Section (12) then the plaintiff can give his complaint in writing to the members of the Grand Jury Panel. If the Grand Jury Panel finds the complaint baseless then it can reject the complaint, or it can order the formation of a new jury panel for hearing the case.
(13.2) According to the complexity of the case and the status of the accused the Grand Jury Panel will decide how many members (between 15-1500) the jury should have. Then the Jury Administrator will form a new jury panel by selecting members by lottery from the voter list and hand over the case to them.
(13.3) Now this jury panel will listen to both parties, witnesses etc. and give a decision. The decision approved by two-thirds of the jury members will be considered the jury’s decision. But for the decision to remove someone from job 75% members’ approval will be required. Every case will have a separate jury panel, and after giving the decision the jury will be dissolved. The press can appeal the decision in the higher jury panel if it wants.
(13.4) If it is proved that the accused is not keeping a gun and cartridges in his house as per the prescribed rules, then the jury members can impose a fine up to 5% of his property (in this property 1 house of 1 crore and furniture up to 10 lakh will not be included) and/or 2 months imprisonment. Except in exceptional cases, for the first offence the fine can be up to 2%, for the second offence up to 4% and for the third and other offences the fine can be up to 5%. The first offence will not be punishable by imprisonment.
14 Vote Recall; District Gun Officer
(14.1) Any voter who has completed 35 years of age can submit an affidavit in person or through any lawyer before the District Collector for becoming District Gun Officer and District Jury Administrator. The Collector will accept his application by charging the same fee as deposited in a parliamentary election and will issue him a specific serial number. The Collector will scan the affidavit and put it on the Chief Minister’s website.
(14.2) Any citizen can go to the Patwari office any day and register “Yes” in support of any candidate for Jury Administrator and District Gun Officer. The Patwari will enter the voter’s “Yes” in his computer and give a receipt. The Patwari will also put the voters’ “Yes” on the district’s website along with the candidate’s name and the voter’s identity card number. A voter can approve a maximum of 5 persons out of the candidates for any post.
1. For registering approval (Yes) the voter will pay 3 rupees fee. For BPL card holders the fee will be 1 rupee.
2. If any voter comes to cancel his approval then the Patwari will cancel one or more names without charging any fee.
3. The Patwari will publicise the candidates’ approvals every Monday and the Collector every 5th date.
Note: The Collector can make a system so that voters can register their “Yes” through SMS, ATM, mobile app.
Range Voting – The Prime Minister or Chief Minister can make a system so that voters can give marks from -100 to 100 to any candidate. If the voter registers only “Yes” then it will be considered equal to 100 marks. If the voter does not register his approval then it will be considered zero marks. But if the voter gives marks then only the marks given by him will be valid. This range voting process is better than the approval system and protects against Arrow’s Useless Impossibility Theorem.

Frequently Asked Questions about an Armed Indian Society
(1) How will this law be implemented in my state?
The provision for referendum is given in Section (1) of this law. The Chief Minister will sign this law and get it published in the gazette and immediately after coming in the gazette the referendum on it will start. Then any citizen of the state can go to the Patwari office or send SMS through his registered mobile and register his Yes on this law. If 55% citizens of the total voters of the state register their consent on it, only then this law will be implemented in the state. Until 55% voters register their consent on it, the referendum will continue. If any voter has registered his consent on this law and he wants to cancel his consent then he can cancel his approval any day. In this way this law will be implemented in the state only after getting the clear majority consent of the citizens, otherwise not.
(2) Can the Chief Minister implement this law without a referendum?
If the Chief Minister wants then he can remove the provision of referendum from Section 1 of this law and implement it directly in his state. If the Chief Minister wants then he can implement this law in some districts or any one district of the state without a referendum. But the writers of this law draft believe that this law should be implemented only after getting the clear consent of 55% citizens, otherwise not.
(3) Why do we need this law?
3.1 External Attack: An armed citizen society is the mother of democracy. Every citizen being a gun owner gives so much power to the state that even after the army loses they can protect themselves and their state.
• Hitler postponed the plan to attack Switzerland only because every Swiss citizen had a gun at that time. When every citizen has a gun then it cannot be defeated by the army, nor can the state be completely controlled.
• Because every Afghan had a gun, America, Britain and Russia could not gain complete control over Afghanistan despite fighting wars for many years.
• Vietnam succeeded in fighting America for 20 years only because after the army lost, the citizens were fighting that war. The Soviet Union sent weapons to the citizens and they could protect their country.
• The British could rule India for 200 years only because Indian citizens were weaponless. The whites had only 1 lakh guns, and with those 1 lakh guns they ruled 60 crore citizens. If even 1% i.e. 60 lakh Indians had guns then the British could not have ruled India.
If today there is a war between India and China, and America refuses to give us weapon help, or sends weapons late, then in the absence of weapons there is more than high probability that our army will break like a wall of sand. And once the enemy army enters our borders then the citizens have no weapons to resist. Then our condition will become like Iraq. This law is necessary to save from such possible crisis.
3.2 Internal Attack: When every citizen has a gun then the country becomes quite safe at the internal level too and there is a reduction in various crimes.
• Kasab could kill dozens of citizens only because the citizens did not have weapons. If Mumbaikars had guns then first of all Kasab would not have come, and even if he came he could not have killed more than 5-7 people. And if such attacks start happening on a large scale in future then we have no remedy.

• Only 2000 armed Islamic terrorists drove out 2 lakh Kashmiri Pandits from the valley. If the Kashmiri Pandits had guns then they would never have had to migrate.
• In 2012 in Kokrajhar, Assam, only 4000 armed Muslim Bangladeshi infiltrators attacked and forced 2 lakh Hindu citizens to leave their land and property and migrate. If all citizens had guns then they would not have had to leave their homes.
• During the 1947 partition also 20 lakh weaponless Hindu citizens had to lose their lives and property. The reason was that the people of the direct action party had weapons while the citizens of the other side were weaponless. Because Sikhs had weapons, they could resist to some extent.
• In India, because of continuous elections, people’s faith in democracy and soldiers’ trust in the government, there has never been a coup till now. But if some foreign power like America etc. wants to get a coup done in India then in just a few months they can create such conditions by starting a civil war, getting terrorist attacks done, creating a feeling of insecurity and showing political helplessness that a general can do a coup.
(4) What changes will come after this law comes in the gazette?
4.1 Negative impact: In the first phase there will be an increase in gun crime like road rage incidents etc. and deaths from guns will increase. But as every citizen gets a gun, in the second phase there will be some reduction in this violence. In this law the right to give permission to any person to keep a gun has been given to the citizens’ jury. Therefore the jury panel will deprive gun criminals of weapons and gun crime will start decreasing. If the Police Chief is also brought within the scope of vote recall passport then the Police Chief will ensure that criminals remain deprived of guns.
4.2 Positive impact:
1. The problem of naxalism and organised crime will reduce by 90% in six months.
2. Crimes like rape, loot, dacoity, kidnapping etc. will decrease by almost 70%.
3. Kasab-like terrorist attacks will decrease. And even if attacks happen there will be less loss of life.
4. Violence related to communal tension and riots will decrease.
5. India will not have to face war with America, China or Pakistan. And even if war happens and our army breaks like a wall of sand then the enemy army will never be able to occupy our land.
6. Technology of gun manufacturing will develop in India and we will become self-reliant in this field.
7. There will be improvement in the behaviour of government officers/leaders and there will also be reduction in oppression. There will be reduction in the oppression being done by police in remote and far-flung areas.
8. There can never be a coup in India like in other countries.
9. In the situation of attack by any enemy country the country will be saved from total loss.
So in our opinion, giving a gun to every citizen is not such a law that will only have benefits. The country will get benefits in many dimensions from it, but there will also be minor loss in one dimension. Because there are both positive and negative sides we have proposed to get a referendum done instead of implementing this law directly.
(5) Guns are quite expensive, how will people buy them?
Seeing British guns, Indian technicians had invented such a design which was better than British guns. Then in 1800 AD the whites took over all the gun factories of India and put a license policy on making guns. And they never used to give licenses. After the 1857 revolt the whites made the Arms Act and also prohibited Indians from possessing weapons. In this law the gun manufacturing factories have been freed from licenses, therefore factories will start being set up on a large scale due to which better and cheaper guns will start being made.

This wrong notion has been created by paid media so that citizens can be kept weaponless. They use one-sided and selective information and create this illusion. When someone’s life is lost because of a gun then it is given large-scale coverage, but those incidents are hidden when guns have protected citizens. This is a real example which proves that the notion – “Giving Indians the right to keep guns will make them kill each other” – is completely false, and has been planted in Indians’ minds by paid media. And most Indians are victims of this notion only because paid media hides the information that 80% citizens of Coorg district have registered guns!! And on the same pattern many news items giving correct information about guns are suppressed, and sensational news is highlighted!!

And when guns reach the hands of law-abiding people then they do not commit crime, but protect from criminals.
3. It is a fact that crimes happen from illegal guns, not from legal guns. If a person has a legal gun then he will not be able to commit crime with it. If he commits crime with a registered gun then he will be caught immediately.

How can you help in getting this law published in the gazette?
1. Please write your name and address, attach a 4 Rs postage stamp and drop it in the letter box on the 5th date at 5 o’clock. Before posting, get one photo copy of the first page of this booklet made.
2. Make one register in the name of “My Demand to Prime Minister Ji”. Before dropping in the letter box, paste the photo copy of the first page of this booklet (which you got made) on the page of your register. Also keep the photo copy of the letter sent to the Prime Minister in the same register.
3. If you are on Facebook then make one album in the name of “My Demand to Prime Minister Ji” and keep the photos of the pages pasted in the register in this album. You can also send a postcard or tweet to the Chief Minister Ji. In the postcard or tweet write: Chief Minister Ji, hold a referendum in the state on the law for keeping guns – #StateGunLawRrp
4. You can send this letter any day. But the writers of this law draft believe that all citizens should send this letter on one fixed date of the month and at a fixed time only. Why fixed date and fixed time?
4.1 If letters are sent on the same day then it will have more impact, and it will also be easy for the Chief Minister’s office to count them. Because the 5th date falls on Citizens’ Duty Day, therefore the 5th date of the month has been fixed for sending letters in the whole country. Therefore you should also send this letter only on the 5th date.
4.2 At 5 o’clock so that the post office staff does not face extra trouble. Usually letter boxes are emptied between 3 to 5 o’clock. Now suppose 100-200 citizens from any city drop letters then they will find the letter box empty, otherwise so many letters will not reach in a full letter box and the post office and citizens will face inconvenience. And after that the postman can empty the post box at 6 o’clock, because very soon they will come to know that the citizens sending instructions to PM/CM drop letters only between 5-6 o’clock. Therefore they will not have to spend extra time in sorting them. So if you send this letter then please drop it in the letter box only between 5 o’clock and 6 o’clock.
4.3 You can drop this letter in any letter box, but as far as possible it should be dropped only in the letter box of the city or town’s Head Post Office. The reason is that the Head Post Office’s letter box is of relatively large size, and the postman does not have to travel much distance to take out the letters from there.
5. If citizens sending letters to PM/CM want to hold any meeting etc. for mutual dialogue then they can hold a meeting on the second Sunday of the month at 4 pm in the evening at the local level. The meeting should always be held at a public place. For this you can choose any temple or railway-bus station premises etc. The second Sunday’s meeting of every month will be at a public place. The time of this public meeting should also remain unchangeable. If the workers want to hold a rally etc. then they can organise a rally on the 4th Sunday of the month at 4 pm in the evening.
6. This is a developed public movement inspired by the non-violence martyr Mahatma Udham Singh Ji. Section (14) of this draft itself is the leader of this movement. If you also want to take this demand forward then do whatever you can at your level. This copy is the last copy, and you can get this booklet printed at your level and distribute it among citizens. To attract the attention of politicians and workers, the most important thing is to contest elections. Therefore to take the demand forward, contest elections for MLA, MP, Councillor, Sarpanch etc. from your area on this issue, or support those candidates who have included this law in their agenda.
