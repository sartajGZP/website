He was humble enough to accept that he had something to learn.

In 1697, Peter I of Russia, later Peter the Great, hit the road on what he called the Grand Embassy. Officially, he was chasing alliances against the Ottoman Empire and dreaming of a Black Sea foothold. But secretly, Peter had bigger plans. He wanted to see Western Europe with his own eyes, study its science, military tricks, and ways of running a country.

To do it, he went undercover as Peter Mikhailov, a regular soldier or carpenter. At 6’8”, blending in was almost laughable, yet it let him wander cities freely, work in workshops, and watch life unfold without the weight of a crown.

He hit Holland, England, Austria, and more. In Dutch shipyards, he swung hammers and learned the ropes of naval construction. In England, he rubbed elbows with King William III, studied the Royal Navy, and picked the brains of thinkers like William Penn. Every scrap of knowledge pumped him full of ideas to build a modern Russian navy and revamp his army.

But Peter wasn’t just after guns and ships. He soaked up culture, urban planning, printing, and crafts, and even brought foreign experts back to Russia. His entourage was a small army of nobles, translators, and craftsmen, showing just how big this mission was. He didn’t nail an anti-Ottoman pact, but he returned home with a vision that would reshape Russia.

Back in 1698, Peter went straight to work, modernizing the army, setting up schools, and eventually building St. Petersburg, a literal window to the West. The Grand Embassy showed that Peter’s genius wasn’t in sitting on a throne. It was in rolling up his sleeves, learning by doing, and building a future from the ground up.
