Why onion futures are banned in usa.

Old Pics:

England, 1955. Two traders, Sam Siegel and Vincent Kosuga, pulled off one of the nastiest market scams in U.S. history. They cornered the onion market—30 million pounds, nearly everything in Chicago—and bet big against the crop. Then they dumped their stash and whispered rumors of a bumper harvest. Prices collapsed. Farmers lost everything, some destroyed their own onions just to cut losses, while Siegel and Kosuga raked in the equivalent of $100 million today.

The outrage was real. Congress held hearings, and in 1958 President Eisenhower signed the Onion Futures Act, banning onion futures trading—a law that still stands. Oddly, it once even banned trading movie box office receipts, though that part didn’t last.
