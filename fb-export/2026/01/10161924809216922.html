TerrifyingMyths:

McMinn County, Tennessee. August 1946. World War II was over. The boys were coming home. But the town they returned to wasn't free. It was run by a political machine led by Sheriff Paul Cantrell. For 10 years, Cantrell ran the county like a dictator. His deputies were thugs. They arrested tourists for "speeding" to steal their money. They beat up anyone who spoke out. They stuffed ballot boxes to stay in power.

The GIs were furious. They had just spent four years fighting fascism in Germany and Japan. They weren't going to accept it in Tennessee. They formed a "GI Ticket" to run for office. Their slogan: "Your Vote Must Be Counted."

Election Day. August 1. The Sheriff knew he was going to lose, so he turned to violence. His deputies beat a Black farmer named Tom Gillespie for trying to vote. Then, they shot a poll watcher. Finally, as the polls closed, the deputies grabbed the ballot boxes and barricaded themselves inside the County Jail. They planned to rig the count in secret, just like always.

But they forgot who they were dealing with. These weren't just angry citizens. These were combat veterans. Bill White, a sergeant who had fought in the Pacific, rallied the men. He said: "If you want those boxes, you'll have to go get them."

The GIs went to the local National Guard armory. They broke the locks. They armed themselves with M1 Garands, Thompson submachine guns, and dynamite. They marched to the jail.

9:00 PM. The veterans surrounded the building. Bill White yelled: "Bring out the damn ballot boxes!" The Sheriff refused. The shooting started.

For six hours, the streets of Athens, Tennessee, looked like a war zone. Thousands of bullets were exchanged. The GIs were disciplined. They kept the deputies pinned down, but the heavy brick walls of the jail wouldn't break. The sun was coming up. They needed to end this.

At 3:30 AM, the veterans taped bundles of dynamite together. They threw them onto the jail's porch. BOOM. The explosion blew the jail doors off their hinges.

The stunned, dust-covered deputies staggered out with their hands up. The Sheriff had fled into the woods. The GIs didn't kill them. They simply walked past them, went into the jail, and carried out the ballot boxes.

They counted the votes right there in the street. The GIs had won by a landslide.

They swore in a new government immediately. They cleaned up the corruption, fired the deputies, and restored the rule of law. Eleanor Roosevelt later wrote: "It is a warning to those who would abuse the power of government."

They proved that the government works for the people. And if it forgets that, the people have the right to remind them.
