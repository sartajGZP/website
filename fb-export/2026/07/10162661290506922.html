History Singapore:

When the prison ship arrived in Singapore harbor in 1825, its cargo wasn't just convicts. It was a desperate colonial government's answer to a labor crisis.

Singapore needed skilled masons, carpenters, and engineers to transform a swampy outpost into a major port.

Free workers were too expensive. So, for nearly 50 years, the British shipped over 15,000 convicts from India.

These men were often political prisoners or skilled artisans convicted under harsh colonial laws. They were put to work building everything.

Their most famous project is St. Andrew's Cathedral. Its brilliant white spires are made from 'Madras chunam,' a plaster they invented from egg whites, lime, and coconut husks.

They also built Government House (the Istana), the Horsburgh Lighthouse, and the drainage systems that made the city livable.

They worked without pay, under the supervision of military officers. When their sentences ended, most had no way to return to India.

They faded into Singapore's growing population, their monumental contributions officially uncredited as the colony celebrated its British architects and merchant founders.
