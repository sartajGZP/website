In the Philippines, the Department of Health (DOH) has asked lawmakers to consider imposing minor penalties on parents who “consciously neglect” to protect their children from vaccine-preventable diseases.

During a House Committee on Health hearing, DOH Undersecretary Emmie Liza Chiong recommended adding provisions to proposed bills on mandatory immunization and the country’s Immunization Agenda 2030. The proposals aim to strengthen routine vaccination programs nationwide.

According to the DOH, only 64.27% of Filipino children in 2025 — or around 1.5 million — were fully immunized, far below the 95% national target. Health officials warned that the country remains at high risk for outbreaks, with immunization coverage hovering between 60% and 70% in recent years.

Lawmakers also raised concerns about vaccine wastage and distribution challenges, particularly in far-flung areas. Meanwhile, discussions expanded to tuberculosis control, with the DOH seeking a larger budget to meet its elimination targets after reporting treatment coverage at 73% of estimated cases in 2024.

Several House bills aimed at strengthening immunization and tuberculosis programs were approved by the committee pending consolidation.

Health officials emphasized that improving vaccination rates and disease prevention efforts is critical to protecting millions of Filipino children and communities nationwide.
