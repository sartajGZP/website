Echoes Of War:

In 1966, as the Vietnam War demanded a constant influx of manpower that the U.S. government struggled to meet, Defense Secretary Robert McNamara launched "Project 100,000," a controversial initiative that drastically lowered recruitment standards to draft men previously deemed mentally or physically unfit for service. These recruits, officially labeled "New Standards Men" but cruelly nicknamed "McNamara’s Mor*ns" by their peers, were often illiterate or suffered from significant cognitive disabilities, making it nearly impossible for them to navigate complex terrain or comprehend life-saving orders. Despite their clear vulnerabilities, they were deployed to the front lines where they perished at a rate three times higher than regular soldiers, serving essentially as "cannon fodder" to fill quotas without jeopardizing college-bound or more affluent youth. This dark chapter in American history remains a haunting example of government cold-bloodedness, where the most vulnerable citizens were sacrificed in a desperate attempt to sustain a failing war effort under the guise of "social reform."
