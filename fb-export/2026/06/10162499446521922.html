In October 2017, a 24-year-old man in Chicago named Dushaun Henderson-Spruce had an idea.

He walked into a post office and picked up a change of address form. The kind anyone can fill out for free. The kind people use when they move house.

He filled it out for UPS... The address of UPS corporate headquarters in Atlanta, Georgia, one of the largest shipping companies on earth. He requested that all mail addressed to UPS headquarters be redirected to his one-bedroom apartment in Chicago.

The form had a line for the submitter's initials. He had written his own before thinking better of it. He scratched them out and wrote UPS instead.

The post office processed the form without question.

Within days, thousands of pieces of mail began arriving at Apartment L2. Letters addressed to the UPS CEO. Correspondence containing personal information about UPS employees. Five American Express corporate credit cards. Business checks. Registered mail. The mail carrier described the volume as voluminous.
For nearly three months, corporate mail for a Fortune 500 company with 500,000 employees continued arriving at his apartment. He deposited more than ten company checks totaling around $59,000 into his personal bank account.

A UPS security coordinator finally noticed the address discrepancy on January 16, 2018. Postal inspectors searched the apartment and recovered approximately 3,000 pieces of mail.

When federal agents confronted him he initially agreed to talk. But when they asked him why he had submitted the change of address form, he ended the interview.

He pleaded guilty to mail fraud and was sentenced to one year in prison.
