.
NEW DELHI: It's a mosquito-repellent all right and chances are, you will probably enjoy using it also.
.
Creams and coils are passe, doctors say fire crackers may be the new route to salvation for dengue-ridden Delhi. And the much-talked about air pollution, which has been the handiest anti-cracker argument in the hands of environment enthusiasts worldwide, could be the key to end the dengue menace.
.
Because the high density of smoke will effectively drive mosquitoes away. In some cases, this may be more efficient than the regular anti-mosquito agents to which, because of round the year use, the insect often becomes resistant.
.
Said Dr Anoop Misra, consultant internal medicine, Fortis Group of Hospitals, "Diwali is watershed time for dengue and two days before it, crackers will start bursting. With smoke density being highest near houses, mosquitoes will definitely be driven away from the residential areas at least." He added: "This is exactly what many smoke-eliciting mosquito devices do — giving a knockdown effect on mosquitoes rather than killing them completely."
.
Thus, he agrees, crackers might signal the beginning of an end to dengue outbreak in the city. Agrees Dr Bir Singh, professor of community medicine, All India Institute of Medical Sciences (AIIMS): "Fire crackers cause pollution, which is harmful for humans. It is equally so for mosquitoes and with the cumulative effect of temperatures reducing at the same time, we can look forward to the dengue outbreak lessening by Diwali time."
