chap-01 : what is missing trader problem ?
------------------------------------------
.
GST law-draft called as Central GST dated 12-apr-2017 has clause-16.2.c, which enshrines " 16. .... (2) Notwithstanding anything contained in this section, no registered person shall be entitled to the credit of any input tax in respect of any supply of goods or services or both to him unless, ....... (c) .... the tax charged in respect of such supply has been actually paid to the Government, either in cash or through utilisation of input tax credit admissible in respect of the said supply; and ... "
.
So whats the efffect of this clause-16.2.c ?
.
Imo, say a buyer B buys goods worth Rs 1000 from seller S, and say GST rate is 20%. Then as per GST-act, seller S must collect, and buyer B must pay GST of Rs 200 GST. And then seller S is supposed to deposit Rs 200 in Govt account, or adjust against his GST payable. But if seller S doesnt deposit this GST amount in Govt treasury, then buyer B (if B is registered vendor, and B is not an end user or small vendor), will defacto have to pay Rs 200 to govt again, because "input credit" will be "denied".
.
This problem in activist circuit is called as MTP = Missing Trader Problem. The economists amd political science professors have refused to give a term to this problem !! As per them, this minor legal / technical problem and has no impact in economy / politics. So economists insist on completely ignoring away the problem, which as per them is non-problem.
.
This problem isnt there in Excise or Income Tax or Wealth tax. In market, the sellers will eventually pass the burden of taxes sellers to pay on the buyers. Say buyer B buys from seller S. Then indirectly, buyers will have to pay burden of taxes S has to pay. And indirectly, or directly, S will collect taxes he has to pay from B,. And say seller S was supposed to pay Excise or Income Tax or Wealth Tax which he didnt pay. Then the Govt will ONLY chase the seller S and buyer B will NOT get harmed in anyway by the Govt. IOW, Excise, Income Tax and Wealth Tax are bipartiate i.e. Govt and tax-payer, while GST / VAT are tripirtaite ii.e. involve Govt, seller, Buyer at the same time.
.
So this is the MTP problem in nutshell - if seller doesnt deposit GST collected in govt then buyer will have to pay GST again to Govt !! And the problem doesnt apply to buyers who are end users (those who take retail invoice) or small traders who dont have GST registration.
.

---- summary of STAS GN ----
.
And the banks will operate an Tax Account as follow
.
(1) The Tax Account will be tied with one and exactly one Current Account in the same bank branch.
.
(2) The interest on Tax Account will be same as Savings Account in that bank branch.
.
(3) The Bank will allow a Current Account owner to tranfer money to his own Tax Account and not any other Tax Account
.
(4) The Bank will not allow anyone to deposit cash in Tax Accounts. Cash mayt be first deposited into Current Account and then transferred into Tax Account.
.
(5.1) IMPORTANT - The Bank will allow Tax Account owner B to transfer funds into another Tax Account owned by S, and it will be presumed as payment of GST paid by B for goods and services he bought from S. B may specify details of invoice against which GST payment is done. And both banks these details.
.
(5.2) IMPORTANT - The Bank will allow Tax Account owner X to transfer funds into Govt Account and will be presumed as payement of GST.
.
(5.3) IMPORTANT - Money from Tax Account cannot be transferred to any Current Account or any account except any Tax Account or any Govt Account meant for payment of GST. If owner of Tax Account wants to money to be deposited into his current account, then he must first deposit into Govt's GST account, and then ask for a refund.
.
(6.1) IMPORTANT - An individual or company may take a digital card such as debit card or credit card, and link the card to his current account which is linked with a tax account.
.
(6.2) IMPORTANT - when owner B wants to pay Rs X to S using debit card, the bank will allow B may specify Rs X1 as payment of goods and Rs X2 as GST (Rs X = Rs X2 + Rs X2). In such case, the card issuing bank must withdraw exactly Rs X1 from current account of B and exactly Rs X2 from Tax Account of B. And it must deposit exactly Rs X1 into current account of S and Rs X2 into Tax Account of S.
.
(6.3) IMPORTANT - when owner B wants to pay Rs X to S using credit card, the bank will allow B may specify Rs X1 as payment of goods and Rs X2 as GST (Rs X = Rs X2 + Rs X2). In such case, the card issuing bank must deposit exactly Rs X1 into current account of S and Rs X2 into Tax Account of S. And later, while accepting payment from B, bank must accept Rs X1 from current account of B and Rs X2 from tax account of B.
.
(7) A bank which is supporting Tax Account may provide cheque which have three lines to denite amounts - first line for bill amount, second line for GST and third line as total. The total must be sum of first two lines or else cheque will be returned. And when such cheque is passed, the money from first line will be withdrawn from Current account and will get only eposited in a current account. And money from second line will be withdrawn from Tax account, and can be only deposited in a Tax Account. The cheque can have additional lines such as invoice number, invoice date etc and bank shall enter and store these data.
.
(8) The bank can also support a feature that in case balance in Tax account is insufficent, then automatica transfer from current account to tax account will be made, so that cheque or debit card payment need not be returned. The current account must have sufficient funds.
.
(9) Banks may put charges to manage the Tax Accounts
.
(10) RBI may make additional rules to comply with GST Act and Banking Act
.

chap-04 : How does STAS stop MTP and GST evasion with just 5 returns per YEAR
----------------------------------------------------------------------------
.
With STAS, each registered vendor had to file just one following QUARTERLY return
.
The return of say 01-apr-2018 to 30-jun-2018 , the vendor can file just one return say on or before 31-jul-2017.

List-1 : List of invoices of goods sold with buyer IDs, sale date, amount buyers paid into Tax Account, date
.
List-2 : List of invoices of goods sold with buyer IDs, sale date, amount buyers paid into Normal Account, date paid
.
List-3 : List of invoices of goods sold with buyer IDs, sale date, amount buyers paid by Cash , date paid

List-4 to List-6 : goods bought, with sellers, same as above.
.

.
Now if payments of GST are done my buyers using Tax Accounts, then entries of buyers and sellers, along with entries given by bank of their Tax Accounts will match. So there is NO need for monthly returns, though buyer/seller and banks may upalod their invoice details anyday before quarter end.
.
All in all, since funds deposited in tax Account never come into Normal account, and eventually go into Govt account, ensures that buyer will never face charge that "you didnt pay GST" or "your seller didnt pay GST" as long as buyer has deposited money directly into Tax Account owned by seller. So there is no need for monthly returns. They can be made optional, but not compulsory.
