(2d) Jurors were given only two choices --- not guilty or judge will decide "life imprisonment or execution".
.
The system in Nanavati case did not give power to Jurors to decide length of punishment,. The Jurors could have said "not guilty" and if they had said "guilty" then judges would have decided the punishment --- be life sentence or execution..
.
The Jurors did NOT have option to say "three years in prison". or "five years in prison"/
.
So Jurors effectively had only two choices - (a) zero punishment OR (b) let judge decide execution or life sentence
.
And execution in this case, where so victim Ahuja was a criminal in first round, and criminal Nanavati was a victim in first round , was perhaps unacceptable to them. And so was life sentence.
.
Basically, Jurors did NOT wish to say "life sentence or execution" . And so they had only once choice --- say "not guilty".
.
IOW, the Jurors only picked the option which they thought was lesser of the two bad options offered to them. If they had choice of "three years in prison", then they may have gone for it.
.
-----------------------

(2e) All in all, IMO the Jurors in Nanavati case could or should have said "three years in prison", but they didnt have that option. They had two choices (i) zero punishment OR (ii) let judge pick life imprisonment or execution. And given the gravity of paternity uncertainity and gravity of adultery 70 years back, Jurors ruled out (b). And so only choice they had was (a).

Today, evaluating the decision of Jurors in Nanavati case is difficult because today, crime of paternity uncertainty and unwanted pregnancy is no more thanks birth control and DNA tests. So today, one may wrongly say "Jurors were not rational and were emotional" etc. But Jurors did apply full mind, and took a rational decision amongst the choices they had
