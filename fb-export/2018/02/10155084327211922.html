Is the stated "core principle" or "guiding principle" give any guide?
.
Say two persons who supporting opposing laws are debating in front of audience. Then many times, during debates, one will state some "core principle" or "guiding principle" and then work to prove that his stand is closer to that "core principle" and so his stand better
.
The question one should put here --- is this guiding principle reducing any dispute? or only creating a cover?
.
eg say two persons are opposing each other on judge vs Jury issue. And say one person says -- "national interest should be supreme" !! Now this guiding principle that "national interest should be supreme" doesnt imo reduce the differences. Because both debators will say that this principle favors their opinion, So one will start proving that "judge system is in best interest" and other will start proving that "Jury System is in best interest". And each will then define national interest in a way that supports his stand. IOW, "guiding principle that 'national interest should be supreme' " doesnt enable audience to take a decision
.
But a core principle like "weapon manufacturing is important" may enable audience to decide. This cant enable him to fully decide, because now one person will try to prove that "judge system improves weapon manufacturing" and other will try to prove that "Jury System will improve weapon manufacturing".
.
But a core principle that "weapon manufacturing is important" imo is more well defined that core principle that "national interest is supreme" because "national interest" has been vague since ages and ages and is vague even now. National interest does give clear picture when two nations are fighting - say India vs Pakistan or India vs USUK or India vs China etc. But "national interest" gives no clear picture when two or more conflicting parties are all inside India such as land hoarders vs manufacturers . But "weapons" and "manufacturing" is relatively well defined compared to broad term "national interest".
.
So question is "is the core principle stated by one debater worth considering"? My answer is --- see if that principle enables audience to decide or not or at least bring him closer to decision if not final decision.

And how is it important for us recallists? Imo, this is useful, because anti-recallists inviduals such as political science professors and congress/rss/aap workers have a looooong list of guiding principle such as "national interest is supreme" or "human rights" or "right to XYZ" , environment etc etc to confuse the audience and let status quo continue. The core principle have have followed are "improve weapon manufacturing", "improve manufacturing", "reduce poverty in a way that manufacturing doesnt decrease" and so on. So when anti-recallist puts his guiding principle to confuse the audience, we need to explain to audience why his principle doesnt enable anyone to make up his mind
