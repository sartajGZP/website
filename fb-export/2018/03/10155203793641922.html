In view of D.I.B's opinion we may request Bengal not to grant a passport to Bose. It is probable that this will evoke a good deal of criticism, particularly as Nehru was allowed to go to China recently, but it seems inadvisable to run any risks.
.
20.10.39
.
My own reaction to the intimation that Subash Bose desired to visit China was to assume that he intended to make contact with Russia which would presumably be to the liking of his Communist friends in the Left Consolidation Committee. I have mentioned the matter to Mr. Caroe and find that he would make the same assumption. Subash Bose might in his view make for Sinkiang and E.A. Dept. would definitely not wish a peripatetic revolutionary to be let loose in Central Asia where he might easily be a potential source of trouble.
.
2. But whether Mr. Bose aims at contacts with Russia or her nominal ally Germany, I agree that he is better here in India where we can watch him and, if necessary, “net” him, than loose in China or somewhere in Central Asia where we can't.
.
3. We may therefore reply to the telegram from the Government of Bengal on the lines proposed. Nehru, whatever his faults, is an entirely different type from Subash Bose and I do not think the precedent of his visit need be regarded as committing us in any way as regards the latter. Moreover the situation has changed since Nehru's visit.
.
21.10.39 (Secretary)

Rahul Arya Ballari Karnataka:

Document reveals the reason why Netaji was denied permission to visit China in 1939 by the then British Indian Govt.
.
Declassified document furnished by Vandana Garhwal show that the authorities in India were apprehensive that Bose might make contacts with Russia. “Whether Bose aims at contacts with Russia...he is better here in India where we can watch him and, if necessary, 'net' him, than loose in China or somewhere in Central Asia where we can't” they concluded. The External Affairs Department, further reads the document, "would definitely not wish a peripatetic revolutionary to be let loose in Central Asia where he might easily be a potential source of trouble".
.
The authorities also refused to consider the precedent of Jawaharlal Nehru’s visit to China the same year, saying that (in their eyes) “Nehru, whatever his faults, is an entirely different type from Subhas Bose”.
.
Then Home Member (forerunner of today's Home Minister) agreed with the decision NOT to allow Bose to visit China.
.
