What is repatriation and what is "repatriation" crisis? How FDI and debt may BOTH worsen repatriation crisis? solution?

======
.
chap-1 : what is repatriation commitment?
.
(1.1) Repatriation commitment is unwritten promise that RBI has given to those who give dollars and get rupees, that when they give rupees back, RBI shall give them dollars as per exchange rate of that day
.
(1.2) Say a company such as say McDonalds wants to do business in India. To do business, it will need to buy / rent land, pay salaries, pay advertisement companies, buy raw materials etc And it will generate sales and may generate profits or suffer losses
.
(1.3) Now to buy / rent land, it needs rupees. But McDonald has only dollars. So McDonald will give say $ 1 crore to RBI (or agency authorized by RBI like say SBI etc) and in turn, RBI will give say Rs 65 crores (say exchange rate is $ 1 = Rs 70) .
.
(1.4) Repatriation commitment on capital --- now an unwritten commitment RBI has given, which RBI has NOT put on net (and you will need to ask CA to verify) is that whenever McDonald gives rupees worth $ 1 crore back to RBI, RBI is "required" to give $ 1 crore to McDonald
.
(1.5) Repatriation commitment on profits --- lets say McDonald earns Rs 20 crore , after taxes, from operations, And it pays Rs 20 crore to RBI.And say exchange rate that say is Rs 65. Then RBI is "required" to give Rs 20 crore / 65 = $ 30.80 lakhs to McDonald.
.
(1.6) Repatriation commitment on capital gains -- lets say McDonald had bought some land for say Rs 5 crore and later sold it for Rs 7 crore. Then Rs 2 crore is capital gains, after taxes. This capital gains is conceptually similar to profit, but legally different. So again, if McDonald gives this capital gains of Rs 2 crore to RBI , then RBI is required
.
So lets add (1.4) , (1.5), (1.6) and subtract losses
.
Say McDonald bought $ 1 crore , and exchange rate was Rs 60 and so it got Rs 60 crores from RBI. It invested that, and after 5 years ,
....... accumlated profits after taxes = say Rs 10 crore
........ capital gains = Rs 15 crore .
say exchange rate = Rs 70
.
Then repatriation commitment on RBI = (Rs 65 crore + Rs 10 crore + Rs 15 crore ) / 70 = Rs 90 crore / 70 = $ 1.29 crore approx
.
i.e. if McDonald were to sell all its assets and decide to wind up, and pay Rs 90 crore to RBI, then RBI as per its unwritten repatriation promise has to give $1.29 crores.
.
But if McDonald had made loses of say Rs 15 crore and capital losses of say Rs 20 crore, then repatriation commitment would be = (Rs 65 crore - Rs 15 crore - Rs 20 crore ) / 70 = Rs 30 crore / 70 = $ 0.43 crore approx
.
So RBI's unwritten commitment to repay dollars INCREASES if foreign company makes profits or capital gains. And repatriation commitment decreases if company makes losses of capital losses.
.
So in case of debt, repayment amount in dollars is fixed --- capital plus interest. But in case of FDI, the repatriation amount depends on profits / losses and capital gains / losses. It may be much much higher than debt, may be less
.
Now FII , FPI etc also create repatriation commitment in same way
.
====================
.
chap-2 : Do we have dollars to give back even initial capital?
.
Now say McDonald doesnt make any profit or losses. and it asks for $ 1 crore back. Then also, will Govt of India be able to repay $ 1 crore ?
.
The answer is NO.. WHY NOT?
.
Because every year, our imports exceed (exports + remittance) . Remittance is when person living in Gulf or USA sends dollars to his own account in India or his relatives' account in India. In case of remittance, govt takes dollars, gives rupees and there is NO repatriation commitment.
.
Why are our imports so high? Well, imo, usuk-elitemen have ensured that leaders dont improve public transport, and make car / scooter buying easier and easier, and leaders destroy footpaths. So card / scooter sale increases and so petrol consumption further rises. And so huge amount of forex is consumed by crude oil import. In addition, govt has kept import duty low. And local manufacturing is poor. So exports are low and imports are high.
.
so consider gap = imports - exports - remittance
.
This gap is covered by net FDI inflow and net debt intake
.
Now for India, for past several years, the imports are so higher than exports, i.e. the gap is so high, that much of the FDI and debt is consumed by this gap.
.
So say McDonald doesnt make any profit or losses. and it asks for only $ 1 crore , that it had given, back. Then also, will Govt of India be able to repay $ 1 crore ?
.
Answer is NO. Because that $ 1 crore would get consumed in importing crude oil etc
.
So whatever dollars that are coming via FDI / debt, get consumed by gap between imports and exports
.
===================
.
(3) total potential repatriation commitment on RBI (approx)
.
Here are some approximate numbers, as in march-2018 (see RBI doc link in comment)
.
(approx numbers)
.
total forex reserves of RBI = $ 430 billion
India's investment in other countries = $ 200 billion
TOTAL India's investment in foreign countries = $ 630 billion
.
total foreign debt = $ 513 billion
total FDI , including unrepatriated profits = $ 400 billion
total other foreign investment (FII, FPI, credits) = $ 200 billion
TOTAL foreign investments into India = $ 1050 billion
.
So if India has to repay debt, and also fulfill all the FDI repatriation and other repatriation commitment, even after paying away entire reserves, there will be short fall of about $ 400.billion !!
.
And that is assuming that India can get dollars back for $ 200 billion it has invested outside India !! That is shaky assumption, because some of the investment is in developing economies like Nepal, Bhutan, Bangladeshi, African countries etc who dont have dollars to repay. So as such, dollar shortfall is much higher than $ 400 billion, it would be above $ 500 billion.
.
=================
.
(4) What is repatriation crisis?
.
Repatriation crisis, is when country runs of out of dollars to repay those to whom it has given commitment to repay.
.
So how does FDI worsen repatriation crisis?
.
As of today in India. the forex that comes via FDI gets consumed in importing crude oil, electrinics etc. So there is no forex left even to repay the capital, if the FDI-bringing-company wants its forex back. Next, FDI-brining companies have superior technology and processes, and so they in general generate more profits. The profit after taxes is repatriable.. So TOTAL POTENTIAL repatriation burden on India increases.
.
So FDI, like FII or debt, increases the possibility of repatriation crisis.
.

.
