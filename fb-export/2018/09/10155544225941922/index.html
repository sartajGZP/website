Declarations vs procedures vs process vs "obvious" demands
.
While writing and evaluating PROPOSED LAW-DRAFTS , all activists, particularly recallists should keep concepts like declarations , procedures, process and "obvious demands" in minds.
.
(incomplete)
.
Many senior activists will put "officer shall bring heaven on earth" or "officer shall make user friendly searchable downloadable website" to create FALSE good impression of drafts. But knowing above concepts of declaration vs procedure vs process helps why such clauses are useless, and in fact such can cause damage
.
------------
.
(a) Declarations or declarative demand --- a clause like "officer shall ensure 24 hour water supply to all" or "officer shall ensure proper education to all" are declarations. Such declarations as comment are tolerable at best. But such declarative demands as binding are not only useless, but higher judges can use these clauses to harass honest officers.. eg today, water supply is just 15 minutes to 1 hour at most. Say a good officer comes and start putting water meters, and he improves water supply to several hours a day. But due to shortage and incomplete metering, he is unable to make it 24 hours. Then in such case, a higher judge can use this "officer shall make 24 hour water supply clause" to harass that good performing officer.
.
Lets say we put clause like "officer shall a citizen verifiable website". Now do words "citizen verifiable" have universally accepted definition? NO. So say officer is a good person, and he makes best possible website within budget.
.
(see also , "Why a clause like "officer shall make a citizen verifiable website" is useless clause and also harmful --- because higher judge can use this clause to harass a good officer" at fb.com/10155548069636922 )
.
Declarations are fine as long as they are very specific and point blank objective. eg say statement like "the fine will be 50% of the taxes evaded, as decided by Jurors". Here, the procedural statement will be "officer shall collect fine equal to 50% of the taxes evaded ...". But declarative statement is very narrow and objective
.
---
.
(b) procedure -- As far as possible, draft should be procedural or may have very narrow well defined objectively defined declarations. But procedure should be so, that costs are well defined and more or less fixed per execution, or below a maximum amount.
.
eg1. "Patwari will enter YES/NO of voter into his PC and collector will ensure that data entered by Patwari will come on PM's website". This is well defined procedure.
.
But consider eg2 "Collector will collect YES/NO of voters in district". Now this is POORLY defined procedure,. Because there are 15 lakh voters in typical district and some 60 lakh voters in a few large districts. Clearly, one person cant collect YES/NO of so many voters/
.
Essentially, if procedure involves HUGE VOLUMINOUS task, then it should be stated as subtasks, where each sub task is small, and something that can be executed 1-3 persons in fixed time and cost.
.
So how can draft writer specify large requirements?
.
Welcome to the concept of "process".
.
-----
,.
(c) process --- Consider task of putting water meters across a district. Putting a binding statement like "officer shall put water meters in all water connections across district" is useless and harmful.
.
