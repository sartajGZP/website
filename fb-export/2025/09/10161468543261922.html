Historyfeels:

In 1992, a 40-year-old Vladimir Putin was photographed carrying bags for St. Petersburg mayor Anatoly Sobchak. Then a relatively unknown figure serving as head of the city’s Committee for External Relations, he would rise within a decade to become Russia’s most powerful man.

At the time, Putin’s role focused on fostering trade deals and international partnerships for St. Petersburg, a city adapting to life after the collapse of the Soviet Union. Before entering politics, Putin had spent 16 years as a KGB officer, primarily in East Germany, before returning to Russia in the early 1990s. His connection to Anatoly Sobchak, a reformist mayor and one of the most prominent democratic voices of the era, proved pivotal. Putin became Sobchak’s trusted aide, handling foreign contacts and sometimes acting as his fixer.

Although his responsibilities were relatively low-profile, the position allowed Putin to build networks and gain experience navigating both political and business circles. When Sobchak lost reelection in 1996, Putin briefly left city politics but soon transitioned to Moscow, where his career accelerated rapidly. By 1998 he was appointed head of the FSB (the successor to the KGB), and in 1999 Boris Yeltsin named him Prime Minister. By year’s end, Putin was Acting President of Russia, just seven years after this photograph was taken.
