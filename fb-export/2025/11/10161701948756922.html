Newly released documents show that Noam Chomsky, the renowned American linguist, author, and MIT professor, maintained "regular contact" with Jeffrey Epstein well after the financier’s 2008 conviction. In the correspondence, Chomsky referred to his interactions with Epstein as “a most valuable experience,” suggesting a deeper relationship than he had previously known.

The documents indicate that Chomsky received roughly $270,000 through an account linked to Epstein, though he insists none of the money came directly from Epstein himself.

Additional records show Epstein arranged meetings with high-profile figures and provided access to discussions and resources that Chomsky appeared to value.

One of the most significant documents released about Noam Chomsky is an undated “to whom it may concern” letter attributed to him, carrying a typed signature and identifying him as a University of Arizona laureate professor, a position he took on in 2017, according to WBUR. The letter references Jeffrey Epstein.

“I met Jeffrey Epstein half a dozen years ago,” the Chomsky letter states. “We have been in regular contact since, with many long and often in-depth discussions about a very wide range of topics, including our own specialties and professional work, but a host of others where we have shared interests. It has been a most valuable experience for me.”
