Brown History:

The “Rawalpindi experiments” were a series of mustard gas tests carried out by British military scientists on the bodies of hundreds of South Asian soldiers. These experiments took place at a military site in Rawalpindi, now in Pakistan, before and during the Second World War. They began in the early 1930s and lasted for more than ten years.

Mustard gas is a dangerous chemical weapon. It causes severe burns when it touches the skin and can lead to long-term illnesses, including cancer. At the time, these long-term effects were not fully understood. As a result, many soldiers suffered health problems long after the experiments ended. The British Ministry of Defence has said it does not know whether the Indian soldiers involved had volunteered for these experiments

The experiments were run by scientists from Porton Down, a British chemical warfare center in Wiltshire. These scientists were sent to the Indian subcontinent to develop chemical weapons that could be used against the Japanese during the war.

Porton Down officials claim that these tests happened in a different time, during wartime, and should not be judged by today’s standards. Today, Porton Down says its main role is to develop defensive equipment to protect soldiers from chemical and biological weapons.

Source: The Guardian, "Military scientist tested mustard gas on Indians." By Rob Evans⁣
