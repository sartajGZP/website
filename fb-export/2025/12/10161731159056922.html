On September 30, 1919, Black farmers in Phillips County, Arkansas, gathered at a church to discuss forming a labor union to secure fair wages for their cotton crops. The Progressive Farmers and Household Union of America (PFHUA) had been organizing to challenge the exploitation faced by sharecroppers on white-owned plantations. This meeting would spark a violent retaliation by white landowners and armed mobs, culminating in what is now known as the Elaine Massacre. Over the following days, a terrifying wave of violence swept through the nearby town of Elaine, killing an estimated 200 or more Black people, though many historians believe the true death toll is still unknown. Federal soldiers were sent to the region, but their involvement was controversial, with accusations that they participated in or turned a blind eye to the massacre. This brutal episode of racial violence remains one of the most horrific moments in American history — a reminder of the lengths to which white supremacy would go to maintain control over Black labor and life.
