A debt is something which has commitment to repay. An investment is something where govt or nation or NO ONE gives any commitment --- the investor is on his own whether he loses or gains. But FDI is interesting cross-breed. When an investor brings say $ 1 billion = say Rs 6000 crore into India, the Govt is giving commitment to repay $1 billion PLUS profits in dollars when the investor gives whole or part of capital + profits back to govt in future. At that time, prevailing exchange rate can be different. But the commitment to repay dollar is there. So FDI is NOT like other investment where Govt does NOT give any commitment to repay or exchange.
.
Example say a person from Mumbai invests in say Delhi. Then Delhi Govt doesnt give him any commitment to repay anything.. But in FDI, there is commitment to repay dollars.
.
This commitment has been fatal in past, and can be fatal in times to come. The commitment becomes fatal when Govt simply doenst have dollars to repay. eg Today, FDI is $360 billion = Rs 360 * 6000 crore = about Rs 21 lakh crores, and debt is $300 billion = Rs 300 * 6000 crore = Rs 18 lakh crores, whole dollars GoI has are only $300 billion = Rs 18 lakh crores. So if foreign investors decide to sell away their capital, and ask for redemption, Govt may not have enough dollars to repay. This will create serious dollar crunch , fall of rupee, rise of all imported goods inclduing petrol and can collapse economy.
